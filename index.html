FIRST THINGS FIRST

So, what actually is AI?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is technology that learns patterns from data to perform tasks that usually need human skills — such as recognising images, understanding language or making predictions.

A chatbot, for example, uses patterns it learned from large amounts of text to generate a reply. It can be helpful, but it doesn't think or understand exactly like a person. And it can get things wrong.

Think of AI as a powerful tool — not an all-knowing brain.

03

THE OTHER SIDE

Cool technology.
Real-world risks.

01

Seeing isn't always believing

Deepfake videos and voices can impersonate real people. In 2024, scammers used fake video-call participants to trick a Hong Kong employee into transferring around HK$200 million.

02

Unfair patterns

If the data used to train AI is biased, its results can be biased too — for example, when helping screen job applicants.

03

Private means private

Putting personal or confidential information into an AI tool can create privacy risks. Pause before sharing.

04

Confident, but incorrect

AI can make up facts and present them convincingly. Check important answers against reliable sources.

04 / LOOKING FORWARD

Tomorrow's AI depends on today's choices.

AI could help with research, accessibility and repetitive tasks. But progress also means thinking about the systems behind the screen.

⚡

Energy use

Data centres need electricity to run and cool AI systems. Growing demand could put pressure on power networks if supply cannot keep up.

☁

Service outages

When a major cloud or internet service goes down, apps and businesses that rely on it may stop working. Back-up plans matter.

◉

Human oversight

AI needs testing, security and people checking important decisions. We should use it thoughtfully, not blindly.

The big question isn't only "What can AI do?"
It's "What should we ask it to do?"
